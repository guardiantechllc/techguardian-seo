## Objection Handling

### "I can just use QuickBooks / Wave / FreshBooks"
> "You absolutely can. But let me ask — do you USE it? Most people pay for QuickBooks and never open it because it's overwhelming. This is Google Sheets. You already know how to use it. And there's no $30-80/month subscription forever. You pay once, it's yours."

### "It's just a spreadsheet though"
> "It's a spreadsheet like a Tesla is just a car. It's fully automated. Formulas, dashboards, auto-calculations, color-coded alerts. You don't touch the formulas. You just type in your jobs. The system does the rest."

### "I can't afford it right now"
> "I get it. But let me ask you this — how much money did you lose last month because you didn't know your real numbers? $500? $1,000? This pays for itself the first month. And I do payment plans on the setup fee if that helps."

### "I'll think about it"
> "For sure. But here's what I've noticed — 'I'll think about it' usually means 'I'll forget about it and keep winging my finances.' Tell you what — I'll send you a 2-minute video of the dashboard. Watch it when you've got a sec. If it clicks, hit me up. No pressure."

### "Can't you just give me the spreadsheet?"
> "I could give you a blank template, but it wouldn't be customized to your business. Your expense categories, your tax rate, your debt, your revenue splits — that's all different from the next guy. The value isn't the spreadsheet. It's the system built around YOUR numbers."

## Closing the Deal

The best close is the demo. Period. When someone sees the dashboard auto-calculate in real time, they sell themselves. Your job is just to get them to watch.

1. Show the dashboard
2. Log a fake job while they watch
3. Show the money split automatically
4. Show the debt payoff tracker counting down
5. Ask: "Want me to build this for your business?"

That's it. Don't oversell. Let the product do the talking.
